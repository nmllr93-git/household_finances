Add statements

Import statements here, or add entries by hand. Once a month has imported transactions for a budget, they replace that month’s sheet rows; a recorded statement balance replaces the sheet balance for that kind of account.

Where do I find my statement?

Sign in to your bank’s website (it’s usually easier than the phone app), open the account, and look for Download, Export or Statements. Choose CSV (or Excel/spreadsheet, saved as CSV), QFX/OFX (Quicken), or download the monthly PDF statement. Pick the date range you want, up to a few months at a time. Menus change from time to time, so the names below may differ slightly.

  • Chase: open the account → the download icon above the transactions → choose CSV or Quicken.
  • Bank of America: open the account → Download above the transactions → Microsoft Excel (CSV).
  • Wells Fargo: Account activity → Download account activity → Comma delimited.
  • Capital One: open the card or account → Download Transactions → CSV.
  • Citi: open the account → Download (or Export) above the transactions → CSV.
  • American Express: Statements & Activity → Download → CSV.
  • Discover: Activity & Statements → Download → CSV.
  • U.S. Bank, PNC, Truist, local banks and credit unions: open the account and look for Export, Download or eStatements. If only PDFs are offered, download the PDF statement.
  • Brokerages and retirement accounts (Fidelity, Vanguard, Schwab and others): download your positions as CSV and use Import positions from a brokerage CSV in the Holdings section, or record the balance from the monthly statement.
  • Mortgage and loans: the monthly PDF statement works; it records the balance and the payment.

Tired of downloading? Turn on automatic bank sync in ⚙ Settings (uses SimpleFIN, about $15 a year).

How the numbers are worked out
  • June–November 2026 history for Nate’s and the shared budget comes from the monthly Google Sheets. Rows keep the month of the sheet they were entered in, even where the date typed was another month. November investment and mortgage figures from the sheets are left out (those sheets still held an earlier month’s numbers); McKenna’s accounts appear once (August), as marked estimates.
  • The range picker counts back from the latest month that has any transaction. “Compare with the previous period” uses the same number of months right before the range.
  • Vendor names are cleaned from the raw descriptions: store numbers, city and state, and card-processor prefixes (SQ *, TST*, PAYPAL *) are dropped, and common merchants are matched by name (all Hy-Vee stores become “Hy-Vee”, AMZN/Amazon Marketplace become “Amazon”).
  • Your rules apply to every transaction whose description contains the text, including history; a later rule wins over an earlier one. An edit to a single transaction wins over any rule.
  • Imported statements: card payments and transfers between accounts are set aside so they aren’t counted twice; refunds reduce spending. Once a month has imported transactions, they replace that month’s spreadsheet rows. Manual entries are counted as typed.
  • Statement check (when you use it while importing): opening balance, plus money in, minus money out, worked out to the cent; for credit cards purchases add to what you owe and payments take it away. It only reports a gap, it never changes your transactions.
  • Settling up: a closed period saves each person’s paid-minus-share as it was then. Payments between people are recorded separately and are not spending.
  • Cash forecast: items marked “estimate” come from your history; “confirmed” bills and paydays you enter replace the matching estimate. Neither is counted in actual totals.
  • Budgets set on this page apply from their starting month onward and replace the sheet’s plan for that category. With rollover on, each month’s leftover (or overspend) since the starting month is added to the next month’s budget. “On pace for” divides this month’s spending by how much of the month has passed.
  • Subscriptions are your Subscriptions category plus known streaming/software services. Recurring bills are other vendors charged at least 3 of the last 12 months at about the same amount. “Per year” is the latest charge × 12 for monthly charges.
